                                                       Abstract
          Sensitive information can cross a security boundary through logs, traces, memory artifacts, caches,
      backups, and integrations even when the primary application response is protected. Ghost Portal
      Cartographer (GPC) analyzes these indirect routes as a finite, declared artifact-version graph without
      inspecting payload contents. Three independently bounded searches distinguish reported scalar-ordered
      lineage, reported lineage with unresolved order, and possible data or unknown-content reachability. A
      positive witness survives resource exhaustion in another search, and the selected witness is labeled by the
      strongest relation it satisfies. Trace parentage remains unauthenticated context, not proof of transferred
      data. A local HMAC demonstration authenticates declared transformation metadata, bound to the
      artifacts and trust domains it describes, under a test policy; it cannot establish sanitization or exclude
      a candidate route. The standard-library implementation was evaluated on synthetic graphs using 171
      tests, two separately coded graph oracles, exhaustive small-graph cases, randomized comparisons, and
      eleven detected deliberate mutations. These results support graph-relative search properties under
      stated assumptions; they do not establish verified exfiltration, complete observation, formal verification,
      or production readiness. Code, fixtures, and falsification conditions accompany the paper.

Keywords: indirect data egress; evidence-tiered lineage; provenance; bounded graph search; observability; repro-
ducibility.

1. Problem and research question

A protected response is not the end of a data lifecycle. Its contents may reappear in exception logs,
distributed-trace attributes, diagnostic memory snapshots, application caches, and configured exports.
OWASP advises against exposing secrets in logs, and OpenTelemetry documents the risk of capturing
private values in telemetry [1, 2]. MITRE separately records weaknesses involving sensitive logs and cached
information [4, 5], and a 2026 empirical study of Android application logs reports private information
written to logs in practice [17]. These mechanisms differ operationally, but each can create another artifact
outside the original control boundary.
A dependency diagram alone cannot establish what moved. A shared trace ID is not an artifact-derivation
record. An enabled exporter is not proof of transmission. Missing telemetry is not proof of safety. Any
useful analysis must separate artifact identity, reported evidence, order, provenance, inventory coverage,
and policy before it assigns a result.
Research question. Can an auditable, metadata-only graph procedure retain reported paths and expose
what remains unknown, without reading sensitive payloads or treating missing observations as proof that
no route exists?
Table 1 lists the surfaces considered here.
Scope. The result is a tested decision procedure for a supplied finite graph. It is not a runtime collector,
a data-loss prevention product, a cryptographic provenance system, a content-aware taint engine, or proof
that a deleted artifact was purged. An edge labeled observed remains a claim supplied by an input source.

2. Contributions and relationship to prior work

GPC separates three questions that are often collapsed into one alert: Does a possible route exist? Is that
route backed by reported artifact relationships? Do the reported scalar sequences admit a strict order?
Three independently budgeted searches answer those questions while retaining stronger positive witnesses,
even if a broader search hits a cap. The output also records path-specific policy, declared coverage gaps,
and diagnostic provenance claims. Predecessor pointers bound path-storage costs. The contribution is
the combination of these decision semantics and an executable test artifact, not a claim to have invented
graph search, cryptographic authentication, or dynamic taint analysis.
Prior systems address important parts of this problem. W3C PROV-DM defines a provenance vocabulary;
CamFlow records system-wide provenance; TaintDroid follows information flow at runtime; OpenLineage
standardizes lineage events for data workflows [6, 7, 8, 9]. GPC does not collect or authenticate their
evidence. OWASP, OpenTelemetry, MITRE, and NIST describe the security risks of records and retained
information [1, 2, 10]. RIFT studies pre-commit information-flow edge admission; GPC instead evaluates
paths already declared in an input graph [11]. W3C Trace Context and OpenTelemetry provide context
propagation, not proof that bytes crossed a boundary [12, 13]. The in-toto framework and DSSE distinguish
authenticated statements from the truth of their contents [15, 16]. Lamport’s work explains why distributed
order is not reducible to wall-clock time [14]. Provenance-based attack investigation is the closest technical
community. Backtracking from a detection point through operating-system dependency graphs dates
to King and Chen [18]; SPADE collects distributed provenance [19]; HOLMES correlates suspicious
information flows into attack stages [20]; NoDoze and UNICORN rank or learn from provenance paths
to reduce false alarms [21, 22]. NovaFabric records agent runs as signed evidence bundles whose lineage
edges are marked as observed or inferred [27], a two-class distinction close to GPC’s mode field. Patent
publications describe taint sets over provenance graphs built from audit records [28], lineage graphs
with sensitive-element classification across streaming platforms [29], and time-ordered lineage for threat
detection [30]. Those systems build and score graphs from collected events. GPC instead takes a declared
graph as given and separates how strongly each returned route is supported, under explicit search budgets.

The term declassification follows the information-flow literature [23, 24], where releasing information is
characterized by what is released, by whom, where and when [25]. GPC deliberately gives a declassification
claim no release effect at all: it is recorded and, when possible, authenticated, but never removes an edge.
Bounded verification that reports an unknown outcome rather than safety when a bound is reached is
established practice in model checking [26]; GPC applies that discipline to three nested relations searched
independently. The present work is a reproducible systems synthesis whose value depends on the limits it
makes visible, not an assertion that earlier approaches are inferior.

3. Threat, trust, and observation boundary

3.1 Protected artifacts, trust domains, and policy

The query names a source artifact s, a declared sensitivity category c, and a destination trust domain
d. GPC does not inspect bytes to confirm the category, and category does not change reachability. The
independent policy function is A(c, d) ∈ {DENIED, PERMITTED, UNSPECIFIED}. An absent rule yields
UNSPECIFIED, not implicit permission. Intermediate assessments describe only the returned witness path;
they are not a regulatory or global authorization decision.
A trust domain is an ownership, authority, or processing boundary rather than a geographic location.
A reported path to a vendor domain is not itself proof that an unauthorized person viewed the content.
Conversely, a supposedly internal destination can still be disallowed by policy.

3.2 Adversaries and collection failures

The adversary may influence instrumentation, metadata, or inventory. The relevant failures include copied
secrets in logs, retained diagnostic artifacts, forged locators, reordered sequences, missing collectors, hidden
destinations, and unrecorded historical copies. The kernel validates declared structure; it cannot verify
that a malicious input describes reality. W3C trace context may originate beyond a trusted boundary [12].
A fabricated edge can create a false route; an omitted edge can hide a real one. Those are limits of input
trust, not defects that graph traversal alone can overcome.

4. Typed graph and three evidence tiers

Definition 1 (Declared artifact-version graph). A finite directed graph G = (V, E) has unique artifact-
version identifiers v ∈ V , each with declared domain dom(v), surface surf(v), inventory annotation
cov(v) ∈ {complete, partial, unknown}, and an optional declared SHA-256 digest h(v). Neither a claimed
complete inventory nor an input digest establishes that physical artifact bytes were checked.

Optional κ is a claimed predecessor edge ID; τ (e) = (traceID(e), spanID(e), parentSpanID(e)) comprises

               EP = {e ∈ E : f (e) ∈ {data, unknown, declassified}},                                               (1)

Here ct-eq compares the computed hexadecimal tag using constant-time comparison. The code uses

The predicate NonceFirstG (e) is scoped to one analyze invocation: after sorting declassified edges by edge

                               ∀e ∈ EP , ∀c,    ExcludeForClaim(e, c) = false.                              (9)

For otherwise identical edge metadata, f (e) = declassified and f (e) = data have identical membership

separate claim-assessment diagnostics differ. The code reports AUTHENTICATED_METADATA_CLAIM or UNVERIFIED_CLAIM;

example a non-hexadecimal tag or an inverted validity window) is treated differently: the loader rejects
the entire input with an error, so no partial result is produced. This fails closed, but it also means one
malformed claim from any party prevents analysis until the input is corrected. Assessments cover every
declassified edge in the supplied graph, not only edges on the selected witness. A future category-specific
exclusion would additionally require a trustworthy semantic postcondition for the transformation and a
model of other residual channels, neither supplied here. An individual IETF Internet-Draft discussing
transformation evidence is relevant context, not an endorsed standard or proof of sanitization [31].
Lemma 2 (Declassified edges behave as data for path existence). In the implemented model, an edge
e with f (e) = declassified has identical membership in EP , ER , EO to an otherwise identical edge e′ with
f (e′ ) = data.

Proof. Equation (1) admits both flow types; Equation (2) admits both when the mode and locator require-
ments hold; and Equation (3) adds only a scalar-sequence filter. Equation (9) rules out authentication-

metadata may differ.

6. Independent bounded searches and decision semantics

GPC runs three independent breadth-first searches: OrderedRoute on EO with state (v, σlast ) and
Equation (4); ReportedRoute on ER with vertex state; and PossibleRoute on EP with vertex state.
All use parent pointers π(x) = (xparent , edge_id) to reconstruct a witness once after success, rather than
copying path tuples at every expansion. OrderedRoute must not merge states solely by vertex because
their future admissible sequences differ.
Each search is independently capped at a positive integer b of dequeued states. The result contains a
witness if one was found, resolution and exhaustion flags, the limit flag, popped and discovered counts,

and coverage gaps on visited vertices. A found path proves existence within its declared relation even if
additional queued states remain. A limit reached without a path says only that exploration was incomplete.
The selector takes the first returned witness in the order ordered, reported, possible, and then re-verifies
that one path against the stronger predicates: edge eligibility and, for the ordered tier, Equation (4)
on its own edges. A reported-search witness whose sequences already increase strictly therefore receives
REPORTED_ORDERED_LINEAGE, even if the ordered search reached its cap first; the result records witness_source
and witness_reclassified. Re-verification inspects only the returned edges and never consults parentage
or claims. If no witness was returned and the possible search finished, the result is NO_ROUTE_IN_DECLARED_GRAPH
even when a narrower search was capped, because by Proposition 1 no narrower route can exist. Figure 2
summarizes the procedure, and Tables 2 and 3 list the outputs.

GPC(query, b):
    require destination_domain != domain(source); require b >= 1
    ordered = BFS_ordered(E_O, source, destination, b)
    reported = BFS_vertex(E_R, source, destination, b)
    possible = BFS_vertex(E_P, source, destination, b)
    # Each search carries its own LIMIT and actual popped count.
    path = first of ordered.path, reported.path, possible.path, or empty
    if path:   status = strongest tier whose predicate path satisfies
                        (ORDERED_LINEAGE > ORDER_UNRESOLVED > POSSIBLE_ROUTE)
    else if possible.exhaustive: status = NO_ROUTE_IN_DECLARED_GRAPH
    else if any(LIMIT):           status = UNKNOWN_LIMIT
    else:                         status = NO_ROUTE_IN_DECLARED_GRAPH
    policy = explicit_policy(category, destination_domain)
    hops = policies_for_each_selected_path_vertex(path)
    coverage = union_of_gaps_at_popped_vertices()
    causal = unauthenticated_context_annotations(path)
    declass = assess_metadata_MAC_claims_without_edge_removal()

7. Mathematical properties and complexity

Proposition 1 (Tier containment). For every well-formed fixed input graph and admissible query,
O(q) ⇒ R(q) ⇒ P(q).

Proof. Every ordered route is a path in EO ⊆ ER , and every path in ER is a path in EP . All three
predicates use the same source and destination-domain condition, and removing the order constraint
cannot invalidate existence.

Scope note (relation versus algorithm). Proposition 1 is a relation-level implication: O(q) ⇒ R(q) ⇒
P(q) is true independently of budgets. Preserving an already returned witness when a different search
reaches its cap is an algorithm-level property, proved separately by Proposition 2.

Proposition 2 (Priority preservation under independent limits). If any search returns a positive witness,
no limit flag from a different search can cause the primary result to be UNKNOWN_LIMIT. An ordered positive
witness is always selected ahead of a reported positive witness, which is selected ahead of a possible positive
witness.

Proof. The selector first inspects the three positive path fields in priority order and only inspects cap flags
when all fields are empty. Independent cap status therefore cannot replace a previously returned witness.
The claim does not say a missing witness in a capped search is absent.

Corollary 1 (Strongest-tier labeling of the selected witness). The primary status is the strongest of
O, R, P whose predicate the selected witness itself satisfies, and it is never weaker than the relation of the
search that returned that witness.

Proof. By Proposition 1 applied to the witness edges, a path satisfying O also satisfies R and P, so the
tiers are nested and “strongest satisfied” is well defined. The selector evaluates the ordered predicate on
the selected path first, then the reported predicate, then the possible predicate. A search returns only

                                                      8
Ghost Portal Cartographer                                                                              Thor


paths in its own relation, so the search relation is always satisfied. A capped search may still miss a
stronger witness that exists elsewhere in the graph; no claim of budget-independent discovery is made.

Corollary 2 (Decisive negative). If the possible search finishes without a witness, then ¬P(q), ¬R(q) and
¬O(q) hold for the supplied graph, whatever the outcome of the other searches.

Proof. Proposition 3 gives ¬P(q); the contrapositive of Proposition 1 gives the rest.

Proposition 3 (Finite-graph exhaustive correctness). When a search resolves without a cap, PossibleR-
oute decides P(q), ReportedRoute decides R(q), and OrderedRoute decides O(q) relative to the
submitted graph.

Proof. Vertex BFS visits every reachable vertex once and records a predecessor for each newly discovered
state. The ordered explorer visits reachable pairs (v, σlast ), accepting exactly edges that satisfy the next
strict inequality. Two ordered states with the same vertex and last sequence admit identical possible
continuations, so deduplicating that pair is sound for existence. In a finite graph, exhaustion reaches all
admissible states. A target is accepted only if its domain is the requested destination and it differs from
the source. The ordered search explores walks; existence of an admissible walk and of an admissible path
coincide, because removing a closed sub-walk leaves a subsequence of a strictly increasing sequence.

Lemma 3 (Returned ordered witnesses are simple). A witness returned by OrderedRoute visits no
vertex twice.

Proof. Breadth-first search returns a minimum-hop admissible walk. If such a walk reached v first with
last sequence σ1 and later left v by edge e with σ(e) > σ2 > σ1 , then e would already be admissible at the
first visit, giving a strictly shorter admissible walk.

Proposition 4 (Edge-addition monotonicity of path existence). With vertices, domains, query, and
existing edge semantics fixed, adding admissible edges cannot remove an already existing P, R, or O route.

Proof. All original route edges, metadata and sequence order remain available. The added edges enlarge the
set of candidate routes without removing existing ones. This does not assert monotonicity of finite-budget
discovery, witness selection, or the primary status under changes to search order or graph size.

Proposition 5 (Conservative non-suppression). For fixed nodes and reported edges, adding, deleting,
authenticating or invalidating well-formed transformation claims cannot remove an EP , ER , or EO path
in the implemented model, provided edge types and graph topology themselves remain fixed. A syntactically
malformed claim causes the whole input to be rejected rather than any path to be removed.

Proof. Equations (1)–(3) depend on the declared graph edge type, mode, locator and scalar sequence, but
not on Equation (7). Equation (9) prevents metadata-claim authentication from deleting an admissible
edge. Therefore the three path-existence predicates are unaffected by a transformation-claim update. The
proposition does not apply to changing an edge’s type, topology, locator, sequence, or historical state.

Scope of Proposition 5. This is a deliberately narrow invariant of this executable kernel, not a general
guarantee for attestation-based declassification. If a future system allowed verified transformations to
exclude an edge, a claim update could change its graph membership and the proposition would require a
new, status-conditioned statement and proof.

Lemma 4 (Ordered state-space bound). Let EO contain the ordered-eligible edges and let OrderedRoute
deduplicate states by (v, σlast ). The number of distinct reachable search states is at most |EO | + 1.


                                                     9
Ghost Portal Cartographer                                                                                 Thor


Proof. The initial state is (s, −1). Every other reachable state is reached through some edge e ∈ EO and
has the form (dst(e), σ(e)). Different edges may yield identical pairs, but deduplication adds a pair only
once. There are at most |EO | non-initial pairs and the initial state.

Complexity. Let n = |V |, m = |E|. Constructing adjacency lists and deterministic edge-ID ordering
takes O(n + m log(m + 1)) time and O(n + m) memory. Each vertex-only BFS has O(n + m) time and
O(n) auxiliary state on top of graph storage; parent reconstruction adds O(ℓ) for a witness of length ℓ ≤ m.
Every ordered state after the start is uniquely determined by the destination vertex and sequence of a last
eligible edge, so at most m + 1 such states exist. Re-scanning outgoing edges at different sequence states
gives a conservative O(n + m2 ) time bound. Re-verifying the selected witness costs O(ℓ). The popped-state
budget does not bound memory: discovered states enter the predecessor map before they are popped.
Parent pointers and queues require O(n + m) graph-plus-search memory, rather than quadratic copied-path
memory. Metadata-claim assessment additionally scans at most m edges, performing bounded-length MAC
comparisons, and causal annotations scan the selected witness once. These are algorithmic worst-case
bounds, not guarantees on interpreter allocation overhead, untrusted input sizes, cryptographic key custody,
or telemetry throughput.

8. Synthetic examples and adversarial counterexamples

real credential. The suffix _v1 identifies a fictional artifact version, not a manuscript release. Table 4 lists

8.1 Counterexample A: correlation is not derivation

Two spans may share a trace ID without exchanging any versioned artifact. Naively connecting them
yields a false path. GPC requires an explicit artifact-to-artifact edge, but remains vulnerable to an input
source that falsely asserts one.

8.3 Counterexample C: unrelated search exhaustion

Suppose reported, strictly ordered edges s → a → t form a two-hop route. Also add many inferred
branches from s into nondestination vertices. With a three-popped-state budget, the ordered and reported
searches can find t, while the broader possible search reaches its cap before dequeuing t. The primary
result remains REPORTED_ORDERED_LINEAGE; the separate possible outcome has limit_reached=true. In a
regression with many parallel s → a edges of increasing sequence, the ordered search instead reaches its
cap first while the reported search returns a path whose own sequences increase; re-verification labels that
path REPORTED_ORDERED_LINEAGE with witness_source=reported. A deterministic regression includes 100,000
reachable inferred branches. This prevents a budget limit from erasing an established witness; it does not
prevent denial-of-service attacks against memory before the cap or against the input supply chain.

8.4 Counterexample D: authenticated assertions do not establish sanitization

A vendor labels an artifact “redacted” and attaches a metadata claim. A forged MAC, incorrect digest,
wrong policy, or a valid claim copied onto an edge between other artifacts is reported as UNVERIFIED_CLAIM.
A synthetic correct HMAC under a configured test identity can produce AUTHENTICATED_METADATA_CLAIM; this
means only that those declared fields were authenticated relative to the local key. Both cases keep the
declassified edge in all relevant route searches. Treating a verified claim as demonstrated content erasure
would create an unsupported negative. Historical copies and other sensitive categories remain unproved.

8.5 Counterexample E: deletion does not imply purging

A cache entry disappears after a vendor forwarding event. Historical copies in telemetry archives, backups,
and third-party systems may survive. The analyzed graph is a supplied snapshot and has no historical
may-have-held state, deletion attestation, revocation enforcement, or physical purge proof.

8.6 Counterexample F: missing collectors and forged evidence

No graph search can discover an omitted connector or independently authenticate a locator string. An
adversary controlling the input file can add, suppress, or relabel edges. The result remains conditional on
input faithfulness; a no-route output must not be translated into an absence-of-egress claim.

9. Reproducible validation and measured boundaries

The kernel and tests require the Python standard library only. The suite of 171 test methods passed
on CPython 3.9.6, 3.10, 3.11, 3.12, 3.13, 3.14 and 3.15.2 under macOS 27.2 on arm64 with network
access denied. One method compares all three relations to a separately implemented DFS oracle on

import the kernel, on another 1,200 graphs. Both oracles were written by the author and encode the

semantic comparison, not independent empirical ground truth.

10. Research limitations and potential falsifiers
 1. Metadata authenticity: observed, parent IDs and trace identifiers are input assertions.
    Evidence strings have no trusted collector signatures, integrity chain or verified origin.
    provenance_trust=unauthenticated is unconditional even when an HMAC verifies a separate trans-
    formation claim.
 2. Causality: Strict scalar order can contradict distributed reality or be manipulated. Parent and
    span identifiers are contextual correlations, not authenticated causal proof; trace ID shape checks
    reject all-zero identifiers but are not comprehensive W3C Trace Context conformance validation.
    REPORTED_ORDER_UNRESOLVED preserves a route hypothesis, not a confirmed chronology.
 3. Open-world uncertainty: Completeness is self-declared. No finite graph search proves absence of
    unmodeled exporters, caches, replicas or vendors.
 4. Content uncertainty and declassification: Neither source category nor declared digests are
    verified against raw bytes. Even successful HMAC checks authenticate only a statement in a local
    demonstration; semantic sanitization, encryption, aggregation and masking cannot be certified. No
    edge is suppressed.
 5. History and revocation: There is no retention-state transition model, deletion attestation, audit-
    grade revocation or historical noninterference proof.
 6. Authorization: Category/domain rules omit principal, purpose, legal basis, time and contract. A
    selected-path policy assessment does not evaluate all paths.
 7. Performance and resource attacks: Three searches incur overhead; ordered exploration has
    quadratic worst-case time. Metadata volumes are input-unbounded beyond the search budget; build-
    time memory must still be controlled upstream.
 8. Information channels: Side channels, inference from aggregates, model memorization and transient
    device state are not analyzed.
 9. Observer privacy: Even abstract path metadata may expose system topology, processing relationships
    and incident context; access controls and safe retention are outside this demonstration.
10. Validation scope: Deterministic regression agreement, two algorithmic oracles, synthetic HMAC tests
    and detected mutants do not constitute formal verification, unbiased ground truth, or independent
    peer review. Demo MAC keys have no secure lifecycle, independent attester or production legitimacy.

The next experiment should use authorized synthetic canaries and independently recorded ground truth.
It should vary clock skew, missing instrumentation, forwarding behavior, and retention while reporting

transformation tests.

11. Conditions for falsification

The claims above are deliberately narrow and admit concrete counterexamples. A reproducible fixed-graph
instance where a complete search omits a route in its declared relation would falsify exhaustive search
correctness. A case where a second capped search overwrites an established positive witness would falsify
priority preservation. A case where matching parent metadata changes the ordered predicate despite
unchanged scalar sequences would falsify the stated separation of correlation and order. A returned witness
that satisfies a stronger predicate but is labeled with a weaker status, or a status stronger than its witness
satisfies, would falsify Corollary 1. An exhaustive negative possible search reported as anything other than
NO_ROUTE_IN_DECLARED_GRAPH would falsify Corollary 2. A transformation claim, verified or not, that causes any
declassified edge to disappear from route eligibility would falsify conservative non-suppression. A successful
metadata-MAC check for a changed payload, a different artifact pair or domain, mismatched policy,
category, declared digest, duplicate nonce, or expired demonstration epoch would falsify the implemented
verification conditions. These are executable kernel-level falsifiers, not claims that can be proved by
counting tests alone.
Deployment-level falsifiers include unauthorized local metadata authentication, while cross-run replay and
independent artifact-byte checking remain unimplemented evaluation requirements. Deployment-level
falsification must separate the tested local checker from unimplemented production controls. In this
local model, accepting an unknown attester or an unauthorized policy, wrong category, altered MAC,
mismatched declared digest or invalid supplied epoch as AUTHENTICATED_METADATA_CLAIM would falsify
its documented checks. Reuse of the same nonce across separate analyses, compromise or rotation of a key,
and a mismatch between declared digests and actual artifact bytes are currently undetectable by design;
any future claim of cross-run replay protection, key lifecycle assurance or byte verification would require
new implementation and independent tests. No valid MAC, including one from a trusted test attester,
establishes that the transformation removed sensitive content.
For real-world validation, an independent authorized laboratory must capture ground-truth artifact
movement (including retained copies and hidden channels) while varying exporters, failed collectors, clock
skew, and transformations. Missed movement would falsify any proposed production detection claim;
such a claim is not made here. All inputs and claimed artifact digests in this paper remain synthetic and
self-declared.

12. Future directions and conclusion

Next work should establish authenticated provenance without confusing span parentage with artifact
derivation; measure category-specific transformation outcomes before considering any flow exclusion; model
historical copies and retention transitions; and compare GPC against provenance, DLP, and taint systems
using authorized canaries. A small machine-checked transition model would strengthen the mathematical
basis. None of these capabilities is present in the current kernel.
GPC is built around a limited but consequential rule: report what the graph can establish, and no more.
Reachability does not establish chronology; chronology does not establish provenance; authentication of a
metadata statement does not establish that sensitive bytes were removed. The implementation preserves
discovered paths across order anomalies and independent search limits, records path-specific policy and
